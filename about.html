About the island

Under 500 square miles, and no two coasts alike.

Taniti is a small tropical island in the Pacific. It covers less than 500 square miles, but the terrain varies a lot across that distance: sandy beaches and rocky ones, a small safe harbor, lush rainforest, and a mountainous interior with a small active volcano.

About 20,000 people live here. Until tourism picked up in recent years, the economy ran mostly on fishing and agriculture, and both are still a large part of daily life on the island.

Most visitors spend their time in Taniti City, which has native architecture and the white sand beaches that encircle Yellow Leaf Bay. Merriton Landing, on the north side of the bay, is developing quickly and is where most of the newer entertainment has opened.

Getting here

Almost everyone arrives by air. Taniti has a small airport that takes propeller planes and small jets, and an expansion is underway that will let larger jets land within a few years. A small cruise ship also docks in Yellow Leaf Bay one night a week.

Transportation on Island

Public buses serve Taniti City from 5 a.m. to 11 p.m. every day, and private buses cover the rest of the island. Taxis run in Taniti City, and there is a rental car agency near the airport. Several vendors rent bikes and helmets — helmets are required by law.

You may not need any of it. Taniti City is flat and very walkable, and the area around Merriton Landing, where many visitors stay, is easy to explore on foot.

Eating and staying

The island has ten restaurants: five serve mostly local fish and rice, three serve American-style meals, and two serve Pan-Asian food. For groceries there are two supermarkets, two smaller stores, and one convenience store open 24 hours.

Lodging runs from an inexpensive hostel up to one large four-star resort, with many small family-owned hotels and a growing number of bed and breakfasts in between. All lodging on Taniti is regulated and inspected by the Tanitian government.

Frequently asked

What currency should I bring?
Taniti uses the U.S. dollar. Many businesses also accept euros and yen, several banks handle currency exchange, and major credit cards are widely accepted.
Will my electronics work?
Power outlets are 120 volts, the same as in the United States.
Is English spoken?
Many younger Tanitians speak fluent English. In rural areas, especially among older residents, very little English is spoken.
What are the alcohol rules?
Alcohol cannot be served or sold between midnight and 9:00 a.m. The drinking age is 18.
Is it safe?
Violent crime is very rare. As tourism has grown there have been more reports of pickpocketing and other petty crime, so take the usual precautions in busy areas.
What about medical care?
There is one hospital, which has many multilingual staff, and several clinics around the island.
Anything to watch for when planning dates?
Taniti has a large number of national holidays, and many attractions and restaurants close for them. Check the dates before you book.
